This is a written program required of every California employer with at least one employee. It must be kept available at the worksite and produced to a Cal/OSHA inspector on request. §1509 requires a construction employer to also adopt a Code of Safe Practices — Hamilton's is a separate posted document, code-of-safe-practices.md, and is part of this program by reference.

1. Responsibility

Alex Li, President is the IIPP administrator and has the authority and the budget to implement this program. He may not delegate that authority away.

Each foreman is responsible for the safety of the crew on his job: holding the tailgate meeting, checking fall protection before anyone goes up, stopping work that is unsafe, and reporting every injury and near miss the same day.

Each employee is responsible for following this program, using the protective equipment Hamilton provides, and reporting hazards. An employee may refuse work he reasonably believes is unsafe. Hamilton does not retaliate for that.

2. Compliance

Hamilton gets employees to follow safe work practices by:

  • Training every employee at hire and again whenever a new hazard shows up.
  • Recognizing crews and individuals who work safely.
  • Retraining an employee who works unsafely.
  • Discipline. Working on a roof without required fall protection is grounds for immediate termination. Other violations follow verbal warning → written warning → termination, depending on severity.

Discipline is documented in the employee's personnel file.

3. Communication

Safety is communicated in a form readily understandable by every employee, in English and in Spanish, because part of the crew's primary language is Spanish.

  • Tailgate / toolbox meeting at the start of every job and at least every 10 working days, per §1509(e). The foreman records the date, the topic, and everyone present on the tailgate log.
  • New hire safety orientation before the employee sets foot on a jobsite.
  • Posted Code of Safe Practices at every jobsite.
  • Anonymous hazard reporting. Any employee may report a hazard to the foreman, to Alex directly, or by text or WhatsApp to the company line, with no name attached and no retaliation.

4. Hazard assessment and inspection

Scheduled inspections happen:

  • Before work starts on every new job — a jobsite hazard assessment covering roof pitch and height, edges and openings, skylights, power lines, ladder placement, access, debris and dumpster placement, and the day's weather and heat forecast.
  • Every day — the foreman walks the roof and the ground before the crew goes up.
  • Whenever a new substance, process, procedure, or piece of equipment is introduced.
  • Whenever Hamilton learns of a new or previously unrecognized hazard.
  • After every injury or serious near miss.

Findings go on the jobsite hazard assessment form and are kept with the job record.

5. Hazards specific to Hamilton's work

Hazard Control
Falls from roofs, edges, ladders, skylights Fall protection at 6 feet — see fall-protection-program.md
Falls through skylights and roof openings Cover and secure, or guard, before work starts
Heat illness Heat Illness Prevention Plan §3395 — see heat-illness-prevention-plan.md
Overhead and underground power lines 10 ft minimum clearance; call before dig; no metal ladders near lines
Ladders 4:1 pitch, 3 ft above landing, tied off, inspected before use
Nail guns and power tools Sequential trip trigger, eye protection, guards in place, no disabled safeties
Falling material, tear-off debris Hard hats, ground control zone, no one under the drop area
Silica and asbestos in tear-off Assume presence in pre-1981 material until tested; wet methods, respirators, do not disturb suspect ACM
Torch-down / hot work on flat roofs Fire watch 60 min after, extinguisher within reach, no torch on combustible deck
Lifting and material handling Two-person lift over 50 lb, hoist bundles, no carrying up a ladder by hand
Driving Valid license, MVR check, no phone in hand, loads secured

6. Correcting unsafe conditions

Unsafe conditions are corrected in order of severity, and as soon as they are found. If a hazard is an imminent danger and cannot be corrected immediately, work stops, everyone is removed from the area except those needed to fix it, and those employees are given the protection they need before they go in.

Every correction is documented: what was found, what was done, who did it, and when.

7. Training

Training is given:

  1. To every employee at hire, before he works.
  2. To every employee given a new job assignment he has not been trained for.
  3. Whenever a new substance, process, procedure, or equipment introduces a new hazard.
  4. Whenever Hamilton becomes aware of a new or previously unrecognized hazard.
  5. To supervisors and foremen, on the hazards their crews are exposed to.

The new hire safety orientation covers, at minimum:

  • This IIPP and how to report a hazard, including anonymously
  • Code of Safe Practices
  • Fall protection: 6-foot trigger, harness inspection, anchorage, tie-off, rescue
  • Heat illness: water, shade, cool-down rest, symptoms, emergency response, acclimatization
  • Ladder safety
  • Nail gun and power tool safety
  • Silica and asbestos awareness for tear-off
  • PPE: what Hamilton provides, when to wear it, how to inspect it
  • Emergency action: how to call 911, how to give the jobsite address, who to call at Hamilton
  • Injury reporting and workers compensation rights

Every training is signed off on the training roster (safety-training-roster.md) and kept. That roster is the record Cal/OSHA asks for.

8. Recordkeeping

Hamilton keeps, for at least one year (§3203(b)) and longer where another rule requires:

Record Retention
Inspection records — date, who inspected, unsafe conditions found, action taken 1 yr min
Training records — employee name, training date, type, trainer 1 yr min (duration of employment + 3 yr preferred)
Tailgate meeting logs 1 yr min
Cal/OSHA Form 300, 300A, 301 injury records 5 yr
Employee exposure and medical records 30 yr (§3204)

Training records are available to an employee or his representative on request.

9. Injury reporting and emergency response

Every injury and every near miss is reported to the foreman the same day it happens. The foreman reports it to Alex the same day.

For a medical emergency: call 911, give the jobsite street address and cross street, send someone to the street to flag the ambulance, then call Alex.

Hamilton reports to Cal/OSHA immediately, and no later than 8 hours after learning of any work-related death, or serious injury or illness (inpatient hospitalization for anything other than medical observation or diagnostic testing, amputation, loss of an eye, or serious disfigurement) — Labor Code §6409.1, at 1-800-963-9424 or the nearest district office.

An injured employee is given the DWC Time of Hire pamphlet and a DWC-1 claim form within one working day of Hamilton learning of the injury.

10. Access

This program is available to every employee. A copy is at the office, a copy is in every company vehicle, and a copy is given to any employee who asks. It is available in English and Spanish.
