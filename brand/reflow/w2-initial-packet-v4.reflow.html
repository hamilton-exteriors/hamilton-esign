1. Employment at will

Employment with Hamilton is at will. Either the Employee or Hamilton may end the employment at any time, with or without cause and with or without notice. Nothing in this Agreement, in any handbook, or in anything said by any Hamilton representative creates a contract of employment for any fixed period or a promise of continued employment. Only the President of ABR Quality Resources Inc may change the at-will nature of employment, and only in a writing signed by both parties.

2. Position and duties

The Employee is hired in the position checked above to perform residential and commercial roofing work, including tear-off, deck repair, underlayment, shingle and flat-roof installation, cleanup, and related exterior work. Jobsites change daily and are located throughout the San Francisco Bay Area and surrounding counties. The Employee will perform the work assigned by the foreman or by Hamilton management, follow all safety rules, and report to the assigned jobsite at the assigned time.

Hours vary with the work available. Hamilton does not guarantee any minimum number of hours, days, or weeks of work.

3. Compensation

3.1 Weekly greater-of compensation. For each Sunday-through-Saturday workweek, Hamilton compares:

  1. the Employee's piece-rate earnings for roofing squares credited to the Employee on completed, approved jobs; and
  2. $16.90 multiplied by all compensable hours in that workweek.

Hamilton pays the greater amount. These are alternative measures of straight-time earnings, not amounts added together. The Employee does not receive a $16.90 hourly base plus the piece rate. If a higher local minimum wage applies to hours worked in a city, Hamilton must make any additional adjustment required by that local law.

3.2 Piece rates. The piece rate depends on the position selected above:

Position Piece rate per roofing square
Roofer $37.50 per roofing square
Foreman $52.50 per roofing square

Squares are credited from the completed job's approved measurement and Hamilton's job records. The Employee will receive written information showing the applicable piece rate and the basis used to determine credited squares.

3.3 Overtime and piece-rate protections. The Employee is non-exempt. California daily, weekly, and seventh-day overtime rules apply: generally 1.5× after 8 hours in a workday or 40 hours in a workweek and for the first 8 hours on a seventh consecutive day, and 2× after 12 hours in a workday and after 8 hours on that seventh day. Overtime is calculated using the regular rate required by law for the workweek, including piece-rate earnings, with any additional overtime premium paid as required.

Paid rest and recovery periods and other nonproductive time are separately tracked and compensated as required by Labor Code §226.2. Rest and recovery periods are paid at the higher of the applicable minimum wage or the average hourly rate required by §226.2. Other nonproductive time is paid at no less than the applicable minimum wage. These items, together with piece units, piece rates, hours, hourly rates, and any guarantee or overtime adjustment, are separately identified on the itemized wage statement as required by law.

3.4 Payday and workweek. Wages are paid weekly on Friday. The workweek and pay period run Sunday through Saturday, and the closed workweek is paid the following Friday. Wage statements are itemized as required by Labor Code §§226 and 226.2.

3.5 Changes. Hamilton may change the rates above going forward. Any change is prospective only, never retroactive, and the Employee will receive an updated Labor Code §2810.5 wage notice within 7 days of the change or on the next timely wage statement.

3.6 Deductions. Hamilton makes only lawful deductions: taxes, withholdings ordered by law, and deductions the Employee authorizes in writing. Hamilton does not deduct from wages for broken or lost tools, damaged materials, vehicle damage, cash shortages, or customer non-payment.

4. Timekeeping

The Employee must record actual start time, meal period start and end, and stop time every day, and must record the city of the jobsite worked each day. Working off the clock is prohibited. Falsifying time records is grounds for discipline up to termination. If a time record is wrong, the Employee must report it before the pay period closes so it can be corrected.

5. Meal and rest periods

The Employee gets an unpaid, uninterrupted 30-minute meal period beginning before the end of the fifth hour of work, and a second 30-minute meal period before the end of the tenth hour when working more than 10 hours. The Employee gets a paid 10-minute rest period for every four hours worked or major fraction thereof. Hamilton relieves the Employee of all duty during meal periods. The Employee must take these breaks and must report to a supervisor immediately if anything prevents taking one.

6. Paid sick leave

The Employee receives California paid sick leave: a minimum of 40 hours or 5 days per year, accrued and used as provided by Labor Code §246. Hamilton does not offer accrued paid vacation or PTO beyond this sick leave. If Hamilton adds a vacation or PTO benefit in the future it will be provided in writing.

7. Tools, equipment, and expenses

Hamilton supplies all tools, equipment, and personal protective equipment required for the work at no cost to the Employee, as required by applicable law. The Employee is not required to buy or provide tools or PPE.

Hamilton reimburses necessary business expenses under Labor Code §2802, including approved mileage when the Employee is directed to drive a personal vehicle for company business between jobsites during the workday. Ordinary commuting to the first jobsite and home from the last jobsite is not reimbursed. Expenses must be submitted with receipts.

8. Safety

Roofing is high-hazard work and safety compliance is a condition of employment. The Employee agrees to:

  1. Follow Hamilton's Injury and Illness Prevention Program, Heat Illness Prevention Plan, and fall protection program at all times.
  2. Wear and correctly use the fall protection, helmet, eye protection, gloves, and footwear Hamilton provides. Working on a roof without required fall protection is grounds for immediate termination.
  3. Report every injury, near miss, and unsafe condition to the foreman the same day it happens, no matter how minor.
  4. Attend and sign off on required safety training before working on a roof.
  5. Never work under the influence of alcohol, cannabis, or drugs that impair the ability to work safely.

The Employee has the right to refuse work they reasonably believe is unsafe, and Hamilton will not retaliate for raising a safety concern or reporting an injury.

9. Workers compensation

Hamilton carries workers compensation insurance covering the Employee. Work-related injuries must be reported the same day so a claim form can be provided within one working day. The Employee has been given the DWC Time of Hire pamphlet and the opportunity to predesignate a personal physician.

10. Driving

If the Employee drives any vehicle for Hamilton, the Employee must hold a valid California driver's license, keep it current, provide a copy, authorize a motor vehicle record check, and report any suspension or revocation immediately. No phone use while driving. Company vehicles are for company business only.

11. Company property and confidential information

Hamilton property, including tools, equipment, vehicles, keys, uniforms, and phones, must be returned on request and on the last day of employment. Customer lists, pricing, measurements, supplier pricing, and job documents are Hamilton's confidential business information and trade secrets and may not be disclosed or used for anyone other than Hamilton.

During employment, the Employee will not perform roofing or exterior work for Hamilton's customers or prospects outside of Hamilton, and will not solicit Hamilton's customers for personal work. This obligation ends when employment ends. Nothing in this Agreement restricts the Employee from working anywhere they choose after employment ends; California does not permit noncompete agreements and Hamilton does not use them.

12. Rights that cannot be waived

Nothing in this Agreement prevents the Employee from discussing wages or working conditions with coworkers, filing a claim with the Labor Commissioner, the Civil Rights Department, the EEOC, Cal/OSHA, or any other agency, cooperating in an investigation, or reporting suspected illegal conduct. Hamilton does not retaliate for any of these.

• The doctor you name in writing before you get hurt on the job

to choose another doctor from the MPN list.

In the event you sustain an injury or illness related to your employment, you may be treated for such injury or illness

by your personal medical doctor (M.D.), doctor of osteopathic medicine (D.O.) or medical group if:

• on the date of your work injury you have health care coverage for injuries or illnesses that are not work

related;

• the doctor is your regular physician, who shall be either a physician who has limited his or her practice of

medicine to general practice or who is a board-certified or board-eligible internist, pediatrician,

obstetrician-gynecologist, or family practitioner, and has previously directed your medical treatment, and

retains your medical records;

• your “personal physician” may be a medical group if it is a single corporation or partnership composed of

licensed doctors of medicine or osteopathy, which operates an integrated multispecialty medical group

providing comprehensive medical services predominantly for nonoccupational illnesses and injuries;

• prior to the injury your doctor agrees to treat you for work injuries or illnesses;

• prior to the injury you provided your employer the following in writing: (1) notice that you want your

personal doctor to treat you for a work-related injury or illness, and (2) your personal doctor's name and

business address.

You may use this form to notify your employer if you wish to have your personal medical doctor or a doctor of

osteopathic medicine treat you for a work-related injury or illness and the above requirements are met.

Unemployment Insurance

Unemployment benefits are paid for by your employer and provide

temporary income when you are unemployed and meet all eligibility

requirements.

Who Should File a UI Claim

You may apply for unemployment if you are out of work or your hours have

been reduced.

To be eligible for benefits, you must have earned enough wages during the

base period to set up a claim, and be:

• Totally or partially unemployed.

• Unemployed through no fault of your own.

• Physically able to work.

• Available for work.

• Ready and willing to accept work immediately.

• Actively looking for work.

When to Apply

You may apply for benefits as soon as you are unemployed, or your work

hours are reduced. Your claim will begin on the Sunday of the week in

which you apply for benefits.

All claims have a one-week, unpaid, waiting period. The waiting period

does not begin until you apply for unemployment, certify for benefits, and

meet all eligibility requirements.

What You Need to Apply

To apply, we need your:

• Name, (including all names you used while working) and Social

Security number.

• Mailing and residence address (if different) and phone number.

• Last employer’s complete name, address (mailing and physical

location), and phone number.

• Last day worked and the reason you’re no longer working (laid off,

quit, fired, or left work because of a trade dispute).

• Work history during the 18 months prior to applying your claim,

including out-of-state employment. Include all employers’ names,

dates employed, and wages earned.

• State-issued driver license number or identification card number.

• Citizenship status and if you have the legal right to work in the

United States. If you indicate you’re registered with the United States

Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS, formerly Immigration

and Naturalization Services) and authorized to work in the United

States, you will be asked for the title of your employment authorization

document and information from the USCIS document, such as the

Alien Registration Number, card number, and expiration date.

Minimum Earnings to Establish a Valid Claim

You must have at least $1,300 in earnings in one quarter of your base

period or at least $900 in earnings in the highest quarter and 1.25 times

your highest quarter earnings in your total base period.

For example: If you have $900 earnings in your highest quarter, you

would also need to have earned a total of $1,125 in the base period

($900 x 1.25 = $1,125).

Unemployment benefits are subject to federal income tax but exempt from

Part-Time Work

If you work less than full-time, you must report that work to us. However,

you may still be eligible for partial unemployment benefits. The first $25 or

25 percent of your gross total earnings for the week (whichever is greater)

will not be counted toward your benefit amount. The amount remaining will

be deducted from your weekly benefit amount .

Example 1:

Your weekly benefit amount is $50. You earn $30. You must report the $30;

however, the first $25 is not counted, leaving $5 to deduct from your weekly

benefit amount. You would receive $45 ($50 minus $5) in unemployment

benefits.

Example 2:

Your weekly benefit amount is $400. You earn $200. You must report $200;

however, the first 25 percent ($50) is not counted, leaving $150 to deduct

from your weekly benefit amount. You would receive $250 ($400 minus

$150) in unemployment benefits.

Appeals Process

If you are denied benefits, you have the legal right to appeal the decision

by completing and mailing the Appeal Form (DE 1000M). To be considered

timely, it must be mailed within 30 calendar days from the mailing date of

the Notice of Determination (DE 1080CZ). If you miss the 30-day deadline,

you may still appeal, but you must show good cause for the delay.

Your appeal will be heard by an independent administrative law judge.

Hearings are informal, but all testimony is taken under oath and is subject

to cross-examination. The office of appeal will let you know when and

where the hearing will be held.

Before the hearing, you have the right to review all records affecting your

appeal. Those records are provided by California Unemployment Insurance

Appeals Board (CUIAB). You can request records from us to prepare for

your hearing.

At the hearing, you may be represented by yourself, a union official,

an attorney, or anyone else you select. You may bring any relevant

documentation to support your case.

After the hearing, you are mailed the administrative law judge’s decision. If

you are not satisfied, you may send a second level appeal to the CUIAB.

For information on how the UI code is applied, including current case

studies, see the Benefit Determination Guide (edd.ca.gov/uibdg).

Important: You must continue to certify for benefits while your claim is

under appeal. If the original decision is reversed, we can quickly issue

all back payments to you. Failure to comply could result in your benefits

being denied or delayed.

California Training Benefits

If you are attending school or training while receiving unemployment

benefits, you may qualify for California Training Benefits (CTB).

If eligible, you can further your education, upgrade your skills, or learn a

new trade while attending an EDD-approved training or school program to

be more competitive in today’s labor market.

While in school or training, you will be exempt from the requirements to

be available for work, actively seek work, and accept work. You may also

be eligible for additional weeks of benefits. To learn more visit California

Training Benefits (edd.ca.gov/en/unemployment/California_Training_

Note: We do not cover educational or training-related expenses such as

tuition, fees, books, supplies, or transportation. However, there are state,

federal, or employer programs that may fund your school or training.

Work Sharing

The Work Sharing program helps participating employers minimize or

eliminate the need for layoffs if the business’s production or services have

been reduced. It allows employers to keep trained employees and quickly

recover when business conditions improve.

To participate, an employer must apply and meet all requirements,

including, but not limited to:

• Be a legally registered business in California.

• A minimum of two employees and at least 10 percent of your

regular workforce must be affected by a reduction of at least 10

percent and no more than 60 percent in both hours worked and

wages earned.

• Health and retirement benefits must stay the same as before, or

they must meet the same standards as other employees who are

not participating in Work Sharing.

• The employees’ bargaining unit must agree to voluntarily

participate and sign the application for Work Sharing.

With Work Sharing, participating employees whose hours and wages have

been reduced can:

• Receive UI benefits corresponding to the percentage of their work

hours and wages.

• Keep their current job.

• Avoid financial hardships.

To learn more, visit Work Sharing Program (edd.ca.gov/en/Unemployment/

Work_Sharing_Program).

Unemployment Insurance Fraud Prevention and Detection

We take the security of personal and confidential information very

seriously. Therefore, all data submitted to us is encrypted. Additional

safeguards are built in to further protect your personal information from

imposter fraud and identity theft.

Imposter fraud occurs when someone intentionally applies for

unemployment using another person’s employment or personal

information. We actively investigate cases of imposter fraud and are

committed to protecting the identities of legitimate claimants.

If we suspect that there may be identity issues, you will receive a written

request to confirm the information provided. We will also contact your

former employers and governmental entities to verify your documents and

information.

For more information, download the Protect Your Identity and Stop

Unemployment Insurance Imposter Fraud (DE 2360EE) (PDF) brochure

(edd.ca.gov/pdf_pub_ctr/de2360ee.pdf).

To report UI fraud, visit Ask EDD (askedd.edd.ca.gov) and select Report

Fraud to send a Fraud Reporting Form online, or call the EDD toll-free

fraud hotline at 1-800-229-6297.

Disability Insurance

Disability Insurance (DI) provides short-term, partial wage replacement

benefits to eligible workers who are unable to work due to a non-work-

related illness, injury, or pregnancy.

When to file a claim: Submit your claim no earlier than 9 days after the first

Exception: If a claim is filed for the same or related cause or condition

Paid Family Leave

Paid Family Leave (PFL) provides short-term, partial wage replacement

benefits to eligible workers who need to take time off work to care for a

seriously ill child, parent, parent-in-law, grandparent, grandchild, sibling,

spouse, or registered domestic partner. Benefits are available to parents

who need time to bond with a new child entering their family either by

birth, adoption, or foster care placement. Benefits are also available for

individuals who need time off work to participate in a qualifying event

resulting from a spouse, registered domestic partner, parent, or child’s

military deployment to a foreign country.

When to file a claim: Submit your claim no earlier than the first day your

family leave begins, but no later than 41 days after your family leave begins

or you may lose benefits.

For faster processing, file your claim using SDI Online (edd.ca.gov/en/

Disability/SDI_Online).

To file, or learn more visit Paid Family Leave (edd.ca.gov/en/disability/paid-

family-leave).

THE FACTS

Sexual harassment is a form of discrimination based on

sex/gender (including pregnancy, childbirth, or related

medical conditions), gender identity, gender expression,

or sexual orientation. Individuals of any gender can

be the target of sexual harassment. Unlawful sexual

harassment does not have to be motivated by sexual

desire. Sexual harassment may involve harassment by a

person of the same gender, regardless of either person’s

sexual orientation or gender identity.

THERE ARE TWO TYPES OF SEXUAL HARASSMENT

1. “Quid pro quo” (Latin for “this for that”) sexual harassment is when

someone conditions a job, promotion, or other work benefit on your

submission to sexual advances or other conduct based on sex.

2. “Hostile work environment” sexual harassment occurs when

unwelcome comments or conduct based on sex unreasonably

interferes with your work performance or creates an intimidating,

hostile, or offensive work environment. You may experience sexual

harassment even if the offensive conduct was not aimed directly at you.

The harassment must be severe or pervasive to be unlawful.

A single act of harassment may be sufficiently severe to be unlawful.

BEHAVIORS THAT MAY BE SEXUAL HARASSMENT

1. Unwanted sexual advances

2. Offering employment benefits in exchange for sexual favors

3. Leering; gestures; or displaying sexually suggestive objects,

pictures, cartoons, or posters

4. Derogatory comments, epithets, slurs, or jokes

5. Graphic comments, sexually degrading words, or suggestive or

obscene messages or invitations

6. Physical touching or assault, as well as impeding or blocking

movements

Employees or job applicants who believe that they have been

EMPLOYER RESPONSIBILITY & LIABILITY

All employers, regardless of the number of employees, are covered

by the harassment provisions of California law. Employers are liable

for harassment by their supervisor or agents. Employees accused of

harassment, including both supervisory and non-supervisory personnel,

may be held personally liable for harassment or for aiding and abetting

harassment. The law requires employers to take reasonable steps

to prevent harassment. If an employer fails to take such steps, that

employer can be held liable for the harassment. In addition, an employer

may be liable for the harassment by a non-employee (for example,

a client or customer) of an employee, applicant, or person providing

services for the employer. An employer will only be liable for this form

of harassment if it knew or should have known of the harassment, and

failed to take immediate and appropriate corrective action.

Employers have an affirmative duty to take reasonable steps to prevent

and promptly correct discriminatory and harassing conduct, and to

create a workplace free of harassment.

A program to eliminate sexual harassment from the workplace is not

only required by law, but it is the most practical way for an employer to

avoid or limit liability if harassment occurs.

Alex Li, President is the IIPP administrator and has the authority and the budget to implement this program. He may not delegate that authority away.

Each employee is responsible for following this program, using the protective equipment Hamilton provides, and reporting hazards. An employee may refuse work he reasonably believes is unsafe. Hamilton does not retaliate for that.

Hamilton gets employees to follow safe work practices by:

  • Training every employee at hire and again whenever a new hazard shows up.

HEAT ILLNESS PREVENTION PLAN

ABR Quality Resources Inc dba Hamilton Exteriors 21634 Redwood Rd Unit F, Castro Valley, CA 94546 · CSLB 1078806 Cal/OSHA 8 CCR §3395 — Heat Illness Prevention in Outdoor Places of Employment

Effective date ______________
Responsible person Alex Li, President — ______________
Applies to All outdoor work. Roofing is a high-heat industry under §3395(e).

§3395 requires this plan to be in writing, in English and in the language understood by the majority of the employees, and to be available at the worksite to employees and to the Division on request. The Spanish version is heat-illness-prevention-plan-es.md. Both go in every company vehicle.

A roof deck runs far hotter than the air temperature. Treat the forecast as a floor, not a measurement.

1. Water

  • Hamilton provides potable drinking water, free, at the jobsite, located as close as practicable to where the crew is working.
  • At least one quart per employee per hour — two gallons per employee for an 8-hour shift — is at the site at the start of the shift, or Hamilton has effective procedures for replenishment during the shift.
  • Water is fresh, pure, suitably cool, and free of charge. Coolers are iced and refilled as needed. Nobody pays for water and nobody is expected to bring his own.
  • The foreman encourages the crew to drink about one cup every 15 minutes and says so out loud, not once at the start of the day.

2. Shade

  • Shade is present and open to the crew whenever the temperature exceeds 80°F.
  • When it is at or below 80°F, shade is provided promptly on request by any employee.
  • There is enough shade for all employees on a recovery or rest period and on a meal period at once, seated normally, without touching each other.
  • Shade is as close as practicable to the work area and does not itself expose anyone to a hazard. A closed vehicle is shade only if it is air conditioned and running.
  • Shade is either a canopy/pop-up Hamilton brings, or a permanent structure or tree that blocks direct sun so the crew can see their skin is not in the sun.

3. Cool-down rest

  • Any employee may take a cool-down rest in the shade of at least 5 minutes whenever he feels he needs it to protect himself from overheating. He does not need permission and does not need to explain.
  • No employee is ordered back to work until the signs and symptoms of heat illness are gone.
  • An employee on a cool-down rest is monitored and asked whether he is experiencing symptoms.
  • Cool-down rest time is paid. It is not a meal period and it is not the paid 10-minute rest period — it is on top of both.
  • If an employee shows or reports symptoms of heat illness during a cool-down rest, first aid or emergency response begins immediately. He is not left alone and he is not sent home alone.

4. High-heat procedures — at or above 95°F

Roofing is one of the industries §3395(e) names. When the temperature equals or exceeds 95°F, the foreman additionally:

  • Maintains effective communication by voice, observation, or electronic means so any employee can contact a supervisor. If a radio or phone is the method, it is tested and it works at that site.
  • Observes employees for signs and symptoms of heat illness, by one of: supervisor or designee observing 20 or fewer employees, a mandatory buddy system, or regular communication with an employee working alone.
  • Designates one or more employees on each site authorized to call for emergency medical services, and lets any employee call when no designated person is available.
  • Reminds employees throughout the shift to drink plenty of water.
  • Holds a pre-shift meeting before work begins to review the high-heat procedures, encourage water, and remind everyone of his right to a cool-down rest.

5. Acclimatization

Heat kills new people. Most California heat fatalities happen in the first days on the job.

  • All employees are closely observed by a supervisor or designee during a heat wave. A heat wave means any day the predicted high is at or above 80°F and at least 10°F higher than the average high of the preceding five days.
  • A new employee, or an employee newly assigned to a high heat area, is closely observed by a supervisor or designee for his first 14 days of employment.
  • New employees are eased in: shorter exposure the first days, more frequent cool-down rests, lighter assignments, and the foreman checking on them by name.
  • Employees are told that symptoms in the first days are expected to be watched for, not hidden, and that reporting them costs them nothing.

6. Emergency response procedures

  1. Ensure effective communication — every crew has a working phone. If a site has no signal, the foreman identifies before work starts where signal exists and tells the crew.
  2. Respond to symptoms. Anyone showing signs of possible heat illness is checked and is never left alone or sent home without being offered on-site first aid or emergency medical services.
  3. Call 911 immediately if an employee shows signs of heat stroke — loss of consciousness, confusion, slurred speech, seizure, hot dry skin, very high temperature. Heat stroke kills in minutes. Do not drive him yourself, do not wait to see if he improves.
  4. Cool him while waiting — into the shade, loosen or remove outer clothing, cool water or ice to the head, neck, armpits, groin, fan him. Do not give fluids to someone who is not fully conscious.
  5. Give clear directions to the site. The foreman knows the street address and nearest cross street before work starts, and sends someone to the street to flag the ambulance.
  6. If the site has no street address, the foreman records coordinates or a clear landmark description before work starts.
  7. Notify Alex Li the same day, and follow the IIPP reporting steps. A heat illness that results in inpatient hospitalization is a serious injury reportable to Cal/OSHA within 8 hours at 1-800-963-9424.

7. Symptoms every employee is trained to recognize

Heat exhaustion Heat stroke — call 911
Heavy sweating, cool clammy skin Hot skin, may be dry, may still be sweating
Headache, dizziness, weakness Confusion, slurred speech, strange behavior
Nausea, vomiting Seizure, loss of consciousness
Muscle cramps Very high body temperature
Fast weak pulse Fast strong pulse

Also trained: personal risk factors — a recent illness, dehydration, alcohol the night before, some medications, age, weight, and not being used to the heat.

8. Training

Before any employee works outdoors, and to every supervisor, Hamilton trains on:

  • The environmental and personal risk factors for heat illness
  • Hamilton's procedures for water, shade, cool-down rest, high heat, acclimatization
  • The employee's right to water, shade, and a cool-down rest, and that asking for them carries no penalty
  • The importance of drinking small amounts often, and of acclimatization
  • Types, signs, and symptoms of heat illness, and the importance of reporting them immediately, in himself or in a coworker
  • That heat illness may progress quickly to serious illness or death
  • Hamilton's emergency response procedures and how to give directions to the site

Supervisors are additionally trained on all of the above before supervising, on the steps to take when an employee shows symptoms, and on how to monitor and respond to weather reports and heat advisories.

Every training is recorded on safety-training-roster.md.

9. Weather monitoring

The foreman checks the forecast high for the jobsite city every morning before the crew leaves, and records it on the tailgate log. If the forecast is 80°F or higher, shade goes on the truck. If it is 95°F or higher, high-heat procedures are announced at the pre-shift meeting and the start time moves earlier where the schedule allows.

FALL PROTECTION PROGRAM — ROOFING OPERATIONS

ABR Quality Resources Inc dba Hamilton Exteriors 21634 Redwood Rd Unit F, Castro Valley, CA 94546 · CSLB 1078806 Cal/OSHA 8 CCR §1669–§1671.2, §1716.2 (roofing operations), §1731 (roofing — residential)

Effective date ______________
Program administrator Alex Li, President
Competent person on site The foreman assigned to the job

Falls are the number one killer in roofing. This program is the part of Hamilton's IIPP that covers them.

1. THE TRIGGER HEIGHT IS 6 FEET

Effective July 1, 2025, Cal/OSHA lowered the fall protection trigger height for residential construction and roofing from 15 feet to 6 feet.

Every employee working at 6 feet or more above the grade or level below must be protected from falling.

  • Slopes 0:12 up to and including 7:12: the trigger is a 6 foot fall distance.
  • Slopes steeper than 7:12: fall protection is required REGARDLESS OF HEIGHT. There is no minimum on a steep roof. Six feet is not a floor you get to work under.
  • Fall height is measured as the vertical distance from the walking or working surface to the ground level below.
  • Parapet height is not included in the roof height measurement.
  • Anyone who learned the old 15-foot rule is working from a number that no longer exists. Do not use it.

Acceptable protection is a guardrail system, a personal fall arrest system, a safety net system, or another system accepted by Cal/OSHA for the work being done.

2. Before anyone goes up — the pre-work assessment

The foreman completes this before the first ladder is set, every job, and records it on the jobsite hazard assessment:

  • Roof height at the lowest eave and at the highest point
  • Roof pitch
  • Roof condition — rot, wet, moss, frost, loose tile, brittle panels, weak decking
  • Every skylight, hatch, chimney chase, vent opening, and hole
  • Edges, including edges hidden by overhanging material
  • Overhead power lines and their distance
  • Anchorage points available, or where anchors must be installed
  • Ladder placement and tie-off point
  • Ground control zone — where material and debris will fall, who is kept out
  • Weather: wind, rain, frost, and the day's heat forecast
  • Which system will be used, and whether the equipment for it is actually on the truck

If the required equipment is not on site, the crew does not go up. There is no version of this job that is worth a fall.

3. Systems Hamilton uses

3.1 Personal fall arrest system (PFAS) — the default on sloped residential roofs

A complete PFAS is anchorage + full body harness + connector (lanyard or self-retracting lifeline) + rope grab and lifeline where used. A body belt is not fall arrest and is never used for it.

  • Anchorage supports at least 5,000 lb per employee attached, or is designed by a qualified person with a safety factor of at least two. Ridge anchors are fastened into structural framing, never into sheathing alone, and the fastener pattern the manufacturer specifies is the pattern used.
  • Free fall is limited to 6 feet and the employee must not contact any lower level.
  • Deceleration distance is limited to 3.5 feet. Total fall clearance below the working level must be calculated before tie-off — lanyard + deceleration + harness stretch + worker height + 3 ft safety margin. If the clearance is not there, use a self-retracting lifeline or a different system.
  • The D-ring is between the shoulder blades, on the back. Front and side D-rings are for positioning and ladder climbing, not arrest.
  • Rope grabs stay above the worker. Lifelines are protected from cut edges.
  • Swing fall is planned out — work directly below the anchor where possible.

3.2 Guardrail systems

Top rail 42 inches ± 3 inches above the walking surface, midrail at about half that, toeboard where anyone can be below. Top rail withstands 200 lb applied outward or downward at the top edge without deflecting below 39 inches.

3.3 Safety nets

Where used, installed as close as practicable under the surface, never more than 30 feet below, drop-tested or certified, and inspected weekly and after any impact.

3.4 Covers for holes and skylights

Every skylight and every hole is covered and secured, or guarded, before work starts near it. A cover supports twice the weight of the employees, equipment, and materials that could be on it, is secured against displacement, and is marked "HOLE" or "COVER" in a color that stands out. A skylight dome is not a cover. Plywood laid loose is not a cover.

Never step or kneel on a skylight, and never carry material across one.

4. Ladders

  • Extend at least 3 feet above the landing surface, and are tied off or otherwise secured at the top.
  • Set at 4:1 — one foot out for every four feet of working length.
  • On firm level footing, or with levelers. Not on buckets, boxes, or unset dirt.
  • Inspected before every use. A damaged ladder is tagged and taken out of service the same day.
  • Three points of contact at all times. Material goes up by hoist or is handed up, never carried up by hand.
  • No metal ladders within 10 feet of an energized power line. Fiberglass only near electrical work.

5. Equipment inspection

  • Before every use, the employee inspects his own harness, lanyard, and connectors: webbing cuts, fraying, burns, chemical damage, stitching, D-ring distortion, hardware cracks, snap hook gate function, and the load-indicator on the shock pack.
  • Any component that has arrested a fall is immediately removed from service and destroyed. It is never returned to the truck, never "checked over and reused."
  • Damaged or suspect equipment is tagged DO NOT USE and given to the foreman the same day.
  • Hamilton documents a periodic inspection of all fall protection equipment and keeps the record.
  • Equipment is retired at the manufacturer's stated service life regardless of appearance.

6. Rescue

Suspension trauma can kill a person hanging in a harness in under 30 minutes. A plan to get him down exists before anyone ties off.

For every job the foreman identifies, before work starts:

  • How a fallen worker will be reached and lowered — ladder, lift, or by 911 response
  • Where the nearest access for a fire department ladder truck is
  • Who calls 911 and who goes to the street to flag them
  • That the jobsite street address and cross street are known and written down

If a worker falls and is suspended: call 911 immediately. Do not attempt a rescue that puts a second person at risk. If he is conscious and it is safe, have him push against the suspension trauma straps or "pump" his legs to keep blood moving until he is down.

7. Training

Before an employee works at height, and again whenever the site, equipment, or rules change, or whenever his behavior shows he did not retain it, Hamilton trains him — by a competent person — on:

  • The 6-foot trigger height and where it applies
  • The nature of the fall hazards on Hamilton's work
  • Correct procedures for erecting, maintaining, disassembling, and inspecting each system used
  • Use and operation of guardrails, PFAS, safety nets, covers, and other protection
  • The role of each employee in a safety monitoring system where one is used
  • The limitations on mechanical equipment on low-sloped roofs
  • Correct procedures for handling and storing equipment and materials, and erecting overhead protection
  • The employee's role in the fall protection and rescue plan
  • Retraining is required whenever changes in the workplace or in the systems used make the previous training obsolete.

Training is certified in writing — employee name, date of training, and the signature of the trainer — on safety-training-roster.md.

8. Discipline

Working on a roof without required fall protection is grounds for immediate termination. This applies to the foreman who allows it exactly as it applies to the employee who does it. There is no production number that justifies an exception and no one at Hamilton has the authority to grant one.

9. Weather stop-work

Work stops and the crew comes down for: sustained wind that makes footing or material handling unsafe, rain, ice or frost on the roof, lightning within audible thunder, or darkness without adequate lighting.
